## What plays shared

An in-game cutscene (the common kind, played in the engine with "Skip part / Skip all") that one machine's quest starts goes on the scene
bus. The other machine starts **its own quest's copy** through its own `CutsceneHandler` (the handler's `EnqueueCutscene` input, the
engine's own trigger), so its quest runs `BeforePlay`, plays, and later gets its own `AfterPlay`. If that machine already played the scene
since its last load, or has no such handler, it plays nothing and its player is a bystander. The second copy's clock is set to the first's
(once, then again only past 0.2 s of drift). A skip on either side (to the next checkpoint, or the end) is sent and done on the other side
too. At the end the joiner's game positions without waiting on the host's people, and his Henry is moved about 1 m off the spot both Henrys
were placed on. Nothing about a scene ever holds or requests a save. Videos, conversations and everything else stay as they were.

## Port notes (another build)

Every call is one WO-193 named; the DLL finds each by what it is: `EnqueueCutscene` by its export; the handler, the configuration and the
in-game cutscene by their RTTI classes (`C_CutsceneHandler`, `C_CutsceneConfiguration`, `C_IngameCutscene`); the movie system by its RTTI
among gEnv's pointers (live: gEnv+0xF0), its three slots (GetPlayingTime +0x1F0, SetPlayingTime +0x200, StopSequence +0x100) checked against
their first bytes; the NPC manager and `C_NPC` by RTTI; the handler's state at +0x338 and its configuration at +0x340 (the export's own
argument is `&handler+0x340`), AutoPlay at configuration +0x20, the "has a time" flag at `C_IngameCutscene` +0xC0. A build whose bytes
differ arms nothing of it and says why.
